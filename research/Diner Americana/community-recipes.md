# Community Lightroom Recipes — Diner Americana

Notes for adapting the **Diner Americana** preset. This is a *night, mixed-source* decade look: 1950s chrome-and-neon diner interiors, warm practicals in the middle of the frame and cool collapse at the edges.

## Reference Looks

- **Edward Hopper, *Nighthawks* (1942)** — fluorescent interior as the only light, warm core against a dark street, viewer outside looking in.
- **American Graffiti (1973) / 1950s roadside neon** — saturated red and blue tube, wet asphalt, chrome speculars.
- **Late-1950s diner interiors** — stainless steel, glass block, terrazzo, Formica, red vinyl, a lit pie case.
- **High Speed Ektachrome night work, c.1959–1965** — 160 ASA, slight magenta-red push, cyan-green ambient.

## Slider Starting Points

| Panel | Range | Contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | 0 to +0.25 | Night density; higher and the "lit box" effect disappears |
| Contrast | +10 to +20 | Chrome speculars and shadow separation |
| Highlights | -25 to -45 | Rescues neon tube and bare bulbs |
| Shadows | +15 to +30 | Counter and booth detail |
| Whites | 0 to +10 | Small hot accents |
| Blacks | -10 to 0 | Keep the floor dark; no grey wash |
| HSL Red sat | +12 to +25 | Vinyl, neon, ketchup |
| HSL Orange lum | +6 to +15 | Warm skin and counter glow |
| HSL Green sat | -10 to -20 | Kills the fluorescent green spike |
| HSL Blue sat | 0 to +8 | Keeps the cyan shadow alive |
| Color Grade highlight | 30–40 hue / 6–12 sat | Tungsten and neon warmth |
| Color Grade shadow | 195–215 hue / 8–15 sat | Cyan-teal, the uncorrected-fluorescent drift |
| Dehaze | -3 to -8 | Interior veiling and glass haze |
| Grain | 0 to 8 amount | Period colour stocks are fine-grained |
| Vignette | -8 to -15 | Seals the frame |

## Common Mistakes

- **Global saturation instead of HSL.** A Saturation +20 on top of the split-tone neutralises the very red/cyan contrast the preset is built on.
- **Shadow saturation past ~20.** 200° at high saturation becomes neon teal and the image reads as synthwave.
- **Warming the highlights with Temperature.** Mixed-source night light is the point; a WB push flattens it into "cosy".
- **Using it on daylight frames.** With no warm source the grade collapses to a cold blue cast.
- **Adding grain.** Mid-century colour reversal film was fine-grained, and 160 ASA night stock was pushed for speed, not texture.
- **Lifting Blacks for "detail".** Shadows +25 already does that; both together produce a milky night image.
