# Dust Bowl Documentary Lightroom Preset — Community Recipes & Settings

Ranges for the 1930s FSA documentary look, based on the file prints in the Library of Congress collection and on how documentary photographers handle dusty, hard-lit conditions today.

## Reference Looks

The authoritative reference is the FSA/OWI file print: warm-toned silver gelatin, pale blank sky, a long midtone run, and grain visible at any enlargement. Contemporary documentary work in the same register (Dust Bowl–era revival projects, farm-crisis reportage) tends to be *less* graded than period-drama imitations: hard sun, minimal colour work, and a straight print. Ken Burns–style treatments sit at the other extreme, and are a good illustration of what to avoid — heavy sepia, artificial vignetting and motion effects turn a documentary record into nostalgia. Reproduction advice from film-preservation sources is relevant too: the archive's warmth is a property of paper and toning, so recreating it as an orange cast misreads the object.

## Slider Starting Points

| Panel | Range | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | −0.4 to 0 | Slightly dense, like a print made for reproduction |
| Contrast | 0 to +20 | The era is not high contrast; resist the S-curve |
| Highlights | −55 to −25 | Holds the pale, near-blank sky |
| Shadows | +10 to +30 | Opens hats, doorways, engine bays |
| Whites | −15 to 0 | Sky should be paper-white, not clipped |
| Blacks | −20 to −5 | Anchor; −30 is the floor |
| Clarity | 0 | Keep at zero when grain is active |
| Texture | 0 | Same |
| Dehaze | −10 to 0 | Negative values model dust-veiled air; use lightly |
| Saturation | −30 to −10 | Dusty, not monochrome; the archive has faded colour too |
| Vibrance | −5 to 0 | Never positive — it re-invents selective colour |
| HSL saturation | Green −40 to −20, Blue −35 to −15, Yellow −20 to 0 | Quiet vegetation, denim and straw |
| HSL luminance | Orange +5 to +12 | Faces and dry earth |
| Color Grade — highlights | Hue 35–45°, Sat 5–12 | Paper warmth |
| Color Grade — shadows | Hue 25–40°, Sat 8–15 | Warm shadow, never blue |
| Grain | Amount 35–50, Size 28–35, Rough 55–70 | Negative plus fibre print |
| Vignette | −30 to −15 | Gentle; the prints are not vignetted hard |

## Common Mistakes

- **Sepia-washing the dust.** The dust was grey-black; the brown belongs to the paper. An orange cast turns reportage into nostalgia.
- **Adding drama.** Positive Clarity and a steep S-curve are the opposite of an FSA print, which is plain, frontal and evenly lit.
- **Crushing shadows.** Faces under hat brims are the subject; `Shadows` negative loses them.
- **Over-graining.** Amount above ~50 with Size above ~35 stops reading as a 4×5 negative and starts reading as pushed 35mm.
- **Dehaze in the wrong direction.** Positive Dehaze removes the atmospheric veiling this look needs; it is also the slider most likely to smear grain if pushed.
- **Modern sky recovery.** Dramatic clouds are a digital-era habit. Period skies are blank because the film held them that way and the lab burned them in.
