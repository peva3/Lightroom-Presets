# Sepia Tone — Community Recipes & Reference Values

> Compiled from darkroom toning threads (Photrio, r/Darkroom), toner kit instructions, and
> published print-toning walkthroughs. Values are translation targets for the Lightroom
> ColorGrade panel, not literal darkroom times.

## Recipe A: "Partial sepia" (highlight-only tone)
- Thiourea or bleach-redevelop sepia, pull the print early (30–40% of full tone).
- Visual result: cream-to-honey highlights, shadows still essentially neutral silver.
- ColorGrade target: HighlightHue ≈ 38–45, HighlightSat 8–12, ShadowSat 0–5.

## Recipe B: "Full sepia" (the archetype)
- Full bleach and redevelop; print 1/4 to 1/2 grade harder to compensate for D-max loss.
- Visual result: rich chocolate-brown throughout, warm highlights, brown-black shadows.
- ColorGrade target: HighlightHue ≈ 30–35, HighlightSat 15–20, ShadowHue ≈ 20–30,
  ShadowSat 10–15, Balance positive (+15 to +30) to weight the tint into the highlights.

## Recipe C: "Sepia + selenium split"
- Sepia full, then selenium short. Warm highlights survive, shadows cool toward aubergine.

## Community consensus points
- Sepia **bleaches**; it lowers D-max. Every experienced printer compensates with more contrast
  and a slightly darker exposure before toning.
- Over-toned sepia is the classic beginner error: milky blacks, no snap. Saturation above ~20–25
  in a digital emulation reproduces that error.
- Hue family is unambiguously yellow-orange-brown (25–45°). Anything landing in the red-magenta
  range is a different process (copper/red toning).
- Unlike selenium, sepia's tint belongs in the highlights — positive Balance is correct.

## Quality assessment
Strong agreement across sources on hue family, highlight weighting, and D-max loss. The most
commonly repeated mistake in digital emulations is lifting the black point too far while chasing
the "old photo" feel.
