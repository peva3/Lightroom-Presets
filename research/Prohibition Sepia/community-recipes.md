# Prohibition Sepia Lightroom Preset — Community Recipes & Settings

Working ranges for the 1920s sepia press-print look, based on period print characteristics and on how contemporary photographers grade flash-lit period material.

## Reference Looks

Two references dominate. The first is the archival one: digitised 1920s press prints and rotogravure pages, where the image is brown-black on cream with visible halftone, and faces are the brightest thing in the frame. The second is period television and film — *Boardwalk Empire* (2010–2014) popularised a warm, low-saturation, slightly lifted-shadow shorthand for the era. The two diverge in one important way: the archive is *lighter and flatter* than the modern grade, which tends to crush the background. Aim between them, closer to the archive. Contemporary flash photographers shooting 1920s-style work (hard single source, bare bulb, close range) produce the most useful modern colour reference.

## Slider Starting Points

| Panel | Range | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | −0.3 to 0 | A touch of underexposure keeps collar whites from clipping |
| Contrast | +10 to +25 | Print density without a modern S-curve |
| Highlights | −50 to −25 | Recovers flash burn on faces and shirtfronts |
| Shadows | +10 to +30 | Smoke-filled interiors need lift, not crush |
| Whites | −10 to +5 | Cream paper, not paper white |
| Blacks | −20 to −5 | Anchor only; −30 and below reads digital |
| Clarity | −10 to 0 | 1920s press lenses were soft wide open |
| Texture | −5 to 0 | Keep it flat; grain carries the texture |
| Dehaze | 0 | Flash-lit rooms have no atmospheric veil — negative Dehaze muddies |
| Saturation | −55 to −35 | The primary sepia move; must be global |
| Vibrance | −5 to 0 | Positive Vibrance re-invents selective colour instantly |
| HSL saturation | Blue −60, Green −60, Aqua −55, Red/Orange −35 to −20 | Wipe the cool end, keep a little life in skin |
| HSL hue | Yellow −8 to 0 | Yellow toward orange warms paper and lamplight |
| Color Grade — highlights | Hue 35–45°, Sat 10–18 | Cream and brass |
| Color Grade — shadows | Hue 25–40°, Sat 12–20 | Brown-black, never blue-black |
| Grain | Amount 30–45, Size 28–35, Rough 55–70 | Sheet film plus halftone dot |
| Vignette | −25 to −10 | Light corner falloff; heavy vignettes look modern |

## Common Mistakes

- **Saturating instead of muting.** A sepia print is nearly monochrome; an orange-washed colour photo is not a 1920s print.
- **Crushing the shadows.** Flash-era prints are lifted and legible. `Shadows` negative plus `Blacks` below −30 turns a speakeasy into a silhouette.
- **Leaving a cyan cast.** If blue and green are not cut hard, shadows go cold and the era reads as "modern teal grade".
- **Negative Dehaze for "smoke".** Flash at three metres does not produce atmospheric veiling; negative Dehaze only lifts the blacks and greys the grain.
- **Over-sharpening.** `Sharpness` above 10 with grain active produces jagged noise — the single most common import failure.
- **Heavy vignette plus heavy grain.** The 1920s reproduction is a *small* image on a large page; two strong effects at once read as an Instagram filter.
