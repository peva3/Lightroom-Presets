# Miami Vice — Community Lightroom Recipes

Community settings for an early-1980s South Beach pastel look, drawn from r/postprocessing and r/AnalogCommunity threads on "Miami Vice colour", cinematography breakdowns of the show's palette, and preset recipes published by colourists who treat it as a period emulation rather than a neon filter.

## Reference Looks

- ***Miami Vice* night exteriors (1984–86)** — magenta neon against cyan ambient, white linen holding the frame's brightest value.
- **Art Deco hotels, Ocean Drive** — pastel stucco, chrome railings, glass brick; the palette's actual source.
- **Daytime boat and dock scenes** — high-key horizon, aqua water, pale sky, low contrast.
- **Memphis Group interiors** — mint, pink and pale grey surfaces with black graphic accents.
- **Michael Mann's later night work** — useful for the cyan-magenta split, though his film palette is harder than the series.

## Slider Starting Points

| Panel | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | +0.15 to +0.40 | Bright by design |
| Contrast | 0 to +20 | Low; pastel dies under strong contrast |
| Highlights | −55 to −30 | White linen and neon cores |
| Shadows | +20 to +40 | Open shade, no blocked detail |
| Whites | +10 to +20 | Clean white |
| Blacks | −10 to +5 | No heavy anchor |
| Temperature | 0 | Do not warm — the grade supplies the colour |
| HSL Aqua | Sat +10 to +20 | Water and pool |
| HSL Magenta | Sat +10 to +25 | Neon and pastel fabric |
| HSL Green | Sat −30 to −10 | No earth tones, no foliage |
| HSL Orange | Lum +5 to +15 | Skin against cyan |
| Color Grading | Highlight 320–340 / 10–18; Shadow 180–200 / 10–18 | Magenta above, cyan below |
| Clarity / Dehaze | −10 to 0 / −10 to 0 | Diffusion bloom |
| Grain | 0 | Broadcast-clean |

## Common Mistakes

- **Pushing contrast.** The show is low-contrast and high-key; +30 contrast turns pastel into a neon poster and clips the magenta.
- **Skipping the green cut.** Without it the preset drifts toward generic teal-and-orange, and any foliage in frame breaks the "no earth tones" rule the design was built on.
- **Warming the white balance.** A temperature push plus a 330° highlight double-warms skin and lands on salmon rather than pastel.
- **Adding grain.** The source is 35mm colour negative transmitted to broadcast; grain reads as a film-emulation tic the show never had.
- **Using Dehaze positively.** Positive Dehaze removes exactly the atmospheric softness the diffusion filtration created.
- **Equal shadow and highlight saturation.** The imbalance — magenta slightly stronger than cyan — is what keeps the frame from looking like a duotone.
